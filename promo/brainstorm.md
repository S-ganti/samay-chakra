# Samay Chakra promo: hook and script brainstorm

## What we are selling (from the repo, not assumed)
- A living 3D world in a browser tab: eight *prahars* (the traditional Indian division of day and night into 8 watches of ~3 h), each drawn from a poster.
- Each prahar has its own raga (Yaman, Bihag, Malkauns, Lalit, Bhairav, Todi, Bhimpalasi, Marwa), light, colour, title in a different Indian script, and a crowd that dances on the beat.
- Generative raga engine, real moon phase, Bengaluru seasonal weather, sounds of each hour.
- Five render styles: Realistic, Fusion Series posters, Sumi & Shu, Chungking Neon, Painted Light.
- Static site, no app, no install: https://s-ganti.github.io/samay-chakra/

## Constraints that shape the hook
- Music-only soundtrack (the site's own raga engine), so the hook must work as **on-screen text + image**, muted or not.
- The research says most drop-off happens in the first 3 s. Target: a hook readable by 1.0 s and the payoff promise by 3.0 s. Vertical cut is ~60 s (your choice), so the end must loop back into the opening.
- Higgsfield scoring was skipped (0 credits), so the hooks below are ranked with an explicit rubric. This is judgement, **not a platform prediction**.

## Rubric (each 0-2)
1. **Interrupt:** frame 1 is a striking image in motion, not a logo or black.
2. **Readable at 1 s:** 7 words or fewer, one idea.
3. **Gap → payoff by 3 s:** opens a question that the next 2 s visibly answer.
4. **Text matches image:** what is said is literally on screen.
5. **Broad audience:** works for non-tech and non-Indian viewers, with a bonus hook for desi audiences.
6. **Honest:** no overclaim the site can't back up.

## Candidates
| # | Hook (on screen) | I | R | G | M | B | H | Total | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | "This isn't a video game." → "It's a clock." | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | **12** | Reframe: the viewer reads it as a game, so we deny it and pay off with a 24 h time-lapse plus dial in 1.8 s. True: the world *is* driven by time of day (there's a real-time clock mode). |
| H2 | "Ancient India split the day into 8 parts." | 1 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 9 | A strong fact hook for desi audiences, but 8 words and needs a diagram to land. Best as **line 2**, not line 1. |
| H3 | "Every 3 hours, this world plays a different raga." | 2 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 7 | Too long; "raga" loses part of the audience at second 1. |
| H4 | "I built a world that lives in your browser tab." | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 7 | Creator-story hook. Weaker gap, better as the closer. |
| H5 | "POV: your clock throws a festival." | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 8 | Trend format, cheap-feeling, slightly off-brand for a poster-art project. |
| H6 | "Your day has 8 chapters. You've seen 2." | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 9 | Good gap, but "you've seen 2" is a made-up claim. |

**Selected stack:** H1 (0-3.2 s) → H2 as the "why" (3.2-6.5 s) → proof cuts → "I built all eight" at ~9.5 s, so the first 10 s carry hook, context and promise.
**Kept as alternates** (cheap to swap later because each line is its own Flick scene): H6 and H5.

## Retention devices
- A 1/8 progress ring in the corner fills as the chapters go by, so the viewer knows how far through they are.
- The music changes at every chapter cut, which proves "each prahar has its own raga".
- The style-flip beat (same shot, 5 styles in 7.5 s) gives a mid-video pattern interrupt around 60%.
- The end card asks "Which prahar are you?" to drive comments, and the last frame matches the first for a seamless loop.
